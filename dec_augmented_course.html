The augmented course: supplementing the learning experience with AI, not replacing it

The DEC course teaches professionals to be augmented, not dependent — to use AI where it removes constraints and keep judgement where it belongs. This briefing applies that same distinction to the course itself: five AI injections that supplement a human-designed learning experience, each one dissolving a cost constraint that made the current design a rational compromise.

The supplement test. Every injection below must pass one question: does it help the learner do the work, or do the work for the learner? Anything that fails is substitution — the dependency the course itself warns against.

Five injections, one repeating pattern

Each stage keeps its human core, adds one AI capability, and changes what the learner experiences. The final line names the constraint that dissolved — the reason this only became affordable now.

Stage 1 · Content

Freshness

Why inject: evidenceCompletion audit (Jul 2026): takeaway PDF diverged from on-screen content; a duplicated scenario; guest interviews ~17 months old, describing AI agents as forthcoming. materials had drifted from screen and from 2026.

What stays

The expert-authored curriculum, frameworks, and guest interviews — the human knowledge is the product.

AI injection

Single-source publishing (one master doc → video script + PDF) and an AI freshness audit that re-reads every claim on a set cadence, flagging dated ones and stamping context notes on older recordings.

Supplemented experience

Material that is current in a field that dates monthly — and a course that visibly practises the source-checking it teaches.

Constraint dissolved: re-verifying every claim monthly was an editorial payroll no lean alliance could carry. AI makes freshness a property, not a project.

Stage 2 · Practice

Rehearsal

Why inject: evidenceCompletion audit: the Module 3 fact-check is a worked example the course performs for the learner; reflective prompts are unassessed and skippable — zero required practice of the core skill. the learner could finish without ever performing the skill.

What stays

The fact-checking exercise concept and the reflective prompts — the pedagogy was right; only the delivery was passive.

AI injection

A Socratic agent hands the learner the flawed output and scaffolds — never answers. The module advances only after the learner makes a call: verifiable or not, what would you check, what is the corrective prompt?

Supplemented experience

You leave Module 3 having caught a hallucination yourself, at your own depth — practitioners move fast, novices get more scaffolding.

Constraint dissolved: one-on-one Socratic coaching used to cost a tutor per learner. An agent gives every learner that tutor at near-zero marginal cost.

Stage 3 · Assessment

Mock & Main

Why inject: evidenceCompletion audit: 3–6 MCQs per module, definitional recall, 80% pass, unlimited retakes — a gate that cannot be failed. Item reuse made retake limits pointless; static item banks were expensive to author. the current quiz is a cost decision, not negligence — small static item banks were all human authoring could afford.

What stays

The module structure, and LEAD plus the course's own frameworks — now serving as the grading rubric itself.

AI injection

Unlimited mock tests with the AI companion (practice, feedback, no stakes) and a gated main test — two attempts per 24 hours, each a freshly generated variant graded by AI against the rubric.

Supplemented experience

Practise until ready, then demonstrate. Failing sends you to a mock with targeted feedback — not a RETAKE button.

Constraint dissolved: marking open-ended judgement and authoring fresh items were both expensive — which is why the unfailable quiz existed. AI grading and variant generation remove both costs at once.

Stage 4 · Issuance

Credential

Why inject: evidenceCurrent certificate is verified by an ID string on DEC's server, issued on the quiz basis above. The course's own Module 4 expert notes a certificate doesn't mean readiness. the certificate was a receipt, verified only by DEC's server.

What stays

The DEC × University of Waikato credential and its brand weight.

AI injection

A signed, learner-held credential (Open Badges 3.0 / W3C VC) that carries the evidence of the AI-graded main-test performance inside it.

Supplemented experience

Proof you hold, verifiable anywhere without asking DEC — and it now attests something that was actually demonstrated.

Constraint dissolved: a signal worth signing. Cryptography was always available; a demonstrated competency to sign is what the assessment injection creates.

Stage 5 · After

Career value

Why inject: evidence1EdTech 2026 ecosystem survey: only ~51% of credential issuers connect directly to employers — the education-to-employment bridge is the ecosystem's weak span. the badge lived on a profile; hiring systems couldn't read it.

What stays

The promise the learner enrolled for — that literacy translates to employability.

AI injection

Machine-readable skills metadata (CLR-style learner records) that AI skills-matching engines in hiring platforms can read and map to roles.

Supplemented experience

The credential participates in hiring decisions instead of decorating a profile.

Constraint dissolved: the certificate speaks machine — skills matching that once required human recruiters reading PDFs now happens at platform scale.

The assessment loop: unlimited practice, gated proof

The design that makes “Literacy for All” and a meaningful certificate compatible: the mock test absorbs all the friction-free access, the main test carries all the meaning.

Mock test · unlimited

Practice space

  • AI companion scaffolds Socratically
  • Rich feedback on every attempt
  • No stakes, no record, no limit
  • Free forever — the access mission lives here
→
Main test · 2 per 24h

Proving ground

  • Fresh AI-generated variant each attempt
  • Find, fix, justify embedded flaws
  • AI-graded against the LEAD rubric
  • Same skills tested, different surface — memorisation can’t pass it
→
On pass

Signed credential

  • Carries the graded evidence inside it
  • Learner-held, verifiable anywhere
  • Readable by hiring platforms
↩On fail: targeted feedback routes the learner back to a mock test aimed at the specific gap — the failure becomes the next lesson, not a wait for the timer.

Why the attempt limit has teeth: a cooldown on a memorisable test only limits speed — wait, remember, grind through. Variant generation is what gives the limit meaning: the test you retake is not the test you failed. The limit and the variants are two halves of one mechanism; either alone fails. (The exact numbers — two attempts, 24 hours — are tuning parameters; the structure is the design.)

Keeping the augmentation honest

Design principles, not caveats — the same standards the course teaches, applied to the course.

The dependency risk applies to us first. An AI tutor that does the exercise for the student recreates the worked-example flaw with fancier technology. The companion scaffolds and questions; it never answers. This is the supplement test, enforced.
Humans moderate the grading. AI marks the main test; a human sample audits the marking. The augmented course keeps judgement in the loop — the same posture it teaches professionals.
Disclosure, dogfooded. Learners are told when AI assesses them and how — LEAD’s own transparency criterion applied to the institution that teaches it.
The critique survives, reframed. The current unfailable quiz was a rational cost decision, not negligence: small static item banks were what human authoring could afford. The injections don’t accuse the design — they dissolve the constraint that produced it. Evidence base: single-learner completion audit, July 2026.

The course teaches professionals to be augmented, not dependent. These five injections ask the course to practise its own lesson — AI where constraints were, humans where judgement is.
